How this was researched ▶

Every number in this tool was pulled from a named, dated source — nothing here is invented. Two categories of figures appear:

Directly sourced facts

Entry-level pricing (official/verified pricing trackers), free-tier terms (vendor free-tier pages and 2026 free-tier guides), and ease-of-use / support-quality numbers (G2 review aggregates, which are real, verified-user survey scores out of 10) are quoted as published. Where sources gave slightly different figures for the same instance (see conflicts below), the most recent, most-corroborated figure was used and the discrepancy is noted.

Calculated / editorial figures — flagged in the UI

The 0–100 "Free Tier Generosity" score is not a number any source publishes directly — G2 and vendor pages don't score free tiers numerically. It's an editorial synthesis built from sourced facts (permanence of the free compute, credit duration, credit value, and monthly hour caps), scored on a transparent rubric explained in each provider's detail panel. It is clearly flagged as an estimate everywhere it appears, per the request that synthetic scores never be presented as sourced fact.

Conflicts found & how they were resolved

AWS EC2 free tier: Sources disagree because AWS changed its policy on July 15, 2025. Accounts created before that date get 750 hrs/month of t2.micro/t3.micro for 12 months; accounts created after get a $200 credit valid for 6 months instead. We report the current (post–July 2025) policy since that's what a new signup gets today, and note the legacy terms in the detail panel.
Google Cloud e2-micro on-demand price: Sources ranged from "~$20/month" to "$20.16/month" for the same 0.028/hr rate. We used the more precise $20.16/month (0.028 × 720 hours) and treat the rounder figures as consistent with it, not a conflict.
G2 "Ease of Use" and "Quality of Support" scores: These vary slightly (by ±0.1–0.2) depending on which competitor a product is being compared against on G2, because G2's comparison pages recompute against the review pool relevant to that pairing. We used the score from the comparison with the largest review sample size (n) for each provider to minimize sampling noise, and cite that specific comparison page.

What we deliberately left out

We did not include "global region availability," "uptime SLA," or "reliability guarantee" as scored criteria in this build — those weren't selected as priority criteria for this comparison's intended audience (solo devs/startups optimizing for cost and simplicity). Numbers for those categories were not researched and are not represented anywhere in this tool.
